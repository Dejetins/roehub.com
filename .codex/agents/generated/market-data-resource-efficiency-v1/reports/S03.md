### M.7 investigation and resolution

Rolling refill removes the former idle barrier and raises sustained contention at the same shared concurrency four. Candidate local admission denials now defer/retry through PG instead of sending 429-producing HTTP attempts. The result is fewer connections (median 1,402→5), zero emulator HTTP 429s (104→0), and improved completed windows, but collector p99 grows 3.04→6.65s (+118.8%). Candidate maximum completion gaps are 8.02–10.19s versus about 5s. All 120 collector requests finish at approximately 357.6–358.1s, before candidate backfill at 399–401s, in every repetition. Each steady one-minute bin contains 19–21 completions, sampled backlog is at most three (baseline two), and oldest pending age at most 6–8s (baseline three). No starvation or growing backlog was observed in this finite fixture; the implementation guarantees neither FIFO admission nor a general cross-runtime wait bound.

This is an explicit throughput-versus-REST-tail tradeoff, not a claim that every guardrail improved. The fixture retries admission every 0.25s; actual durable collector repair uses different retry/backoff (including 15s), so these HTTP-only timings do not establish live receive-to-canonical, repair or API latency. S02's live/stream p99 median +14.6% inconclusive result is carried forward unchanged, not repaired or re-proved by S03. No browser-visible API/DTO behavior was changed; no browser QA pass is claimed.

PG transaction totals rise about sixfold (10,885→65,380), and mean whole-run transaction rate rises about elevenfold (14.8→163.6 tx/s). Connections peak at seven versus four. This is a material capacity cost of shared admission/session checks, not overall lower infrastructure load. The bounded fixture completed without coordination outage or quota breach under the same PG cap. No production PG capacity or latency bound is established. Host family RSS rises ~35%; sampled CH cgroup peak rises ~9.5% with overlapping ranges and remains below the fixed cap. CH IO spread is wide: no IO improvement claim. These costs are retained as successor inputs. Independent review accepted the reasoned M.7 resolution without inventing an owner SLO or treating unspecified thresholds as a waiver.

### Discovered memory defect and final repair

An earlier transport strategy spawned a full scheduler import for each HTTP child. A Linux probe under the actual unchanged 384MiB/1CPU scheduler cap OOM-killed the process (exit 137) while creating the second child. `linux-memory-probe-01.json` and raw companions preserve the failure. M3 was stopped after its completed B1/C1 pair; no partial series was promoted.

The repair uses the lean stdlib/requests leaf worker and bounded exclusive pool. `linux-memory-probe-02.json` tests six callers and a stricter 26 callers (actual Python default scheduler executor 22 plus four backfill), with four retained children and four held 1,000-row responses. Peaks 238,080,000/240,689,152 bytes remain below 384MiB; all futures settle, all children exit/join, permits return to zero, OOM events remain zero. This closes the demonstrated HTTP-retention/import OOM boundary. It does not prove the full CH-writing scheduler working set at production load. Limits were not raised.

M1/M2 abort records preserve the earlier Bybit long-Retry-After correction and measurement/accounting gaps. M3 is exploratory only. M4 is the sole final comparable series. All failed/interrupted artifacts remain. Two early pytest artifacts exposed the disposable PG password in a dataclass repr and were redacted in place; subsequent repr suppression and output redaction prevent recurrence.
